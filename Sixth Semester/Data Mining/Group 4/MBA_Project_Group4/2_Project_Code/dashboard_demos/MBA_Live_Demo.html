S

Support

How often does this combination appear across ALL transactions?

Support(A→B) = freq(A∪B) / N

Example: 100 of 1000 receipts had milk+bread → Support = 0.10

Our threshold: ≥ 0.01 (appears in at least 1% of baskets)

C

Confidence

When someone buys A, how often do they ALSO buy B?

Confidence(A→B) = freq(A∪B) / freq(A)

Example: 100 bought milk+bread, 200 bought milk → Confidence = 50%

Our threshold: ≥ 0.20 (rule correct at least 20% of time)

L

Lift

Is this pattern real or just coincidence?

Lift(A→B) = Confidence / P(B)

Lift = 1 → coincidence. Lift > 1 → real. Lift > 3 → strong. Lift > 10 → exceptional.

Your Avg Lift = 10.57 — Exceptional
K

Conviction

Filters out accidental/redundant rules. Higher = stronger rule direction.

Conviction = (1 - P(B)) / (1 - Confidence)

We used ≥ 1.1 to prune weak rules — directly addresses Gap 2.

R

RFM Segmentation

Score every customer on 3 dimensions:

R = Recency (days since last buy)
F = Frequency (how many orders)
M = Monetary (total spend)

Each scored 1–3, summed to 3–9. ≥8 = High Value, ≥5 = Medium, else Low.

A

Apriori Algorithm

Works in passes over the transaction data:

Pass 1: Find frequent single items
Pass 2: Build frequent pairs
Pass 3: Build frequent triplets

Anti-monotone property — if {A,B} is infrequent, no superset can be frequent. Prunes search space massively.
